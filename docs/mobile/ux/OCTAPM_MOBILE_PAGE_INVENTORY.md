# OctaPM Mobile — Page Inventory

**Audience:** whoever plans the implementation waves, and the reviewer checking that nothing was quietly dropped.
**Date:** 2026-09-19. Companion to the [UX audit](OCTAPM_MOBILE_UX_AUDIT.md) and the [V2 spec](OCTAPM_MOBILE_UX_V2_SPEC.md).

## 1. The numbers, and an honest word about scope

| | |
|---|---|
| Route files today | **70** (`mobile/app/**/*.tsx`, excluding layouts) |
| Screens audited (states and sub-screens) | **107** |
| KEEP · EXPAND · REDESIGN | **2 · 37 · 68** |
| Screens the audit says should exist and do not | **148** |
| Screens §62 of the brief names | **~35** |
| Needs the backend cannot serve | **212** |

**148 is not a plan; it is a finding.** Twelve auditors working independently asked for 148 screens, and the brief names about 35. The overlap is where the signal is: a **role home**, a **location browser**, a **real daily site report**, a **photo-first capture screen** and **related-records on every detail page** were each asked for independently by four or more domains that could not see each other's work. Those are not opinions, they are the same hole seen from five directions.

The rest is a backlog, and this phase does not pretend otherwise. The waves in the [V2 spec](OCTAPM_MOBILE_UX_V2_SPEC.md) take the brief's ~35 plus the independently-corroborated additions; everything else in §3 below is recorded so that it is *chosen against* rather than forgotten. A phase that tried to ship 148 new screens would ship none of them well.

## 2. How to read the verdicts

**KEEP** the screen is right · **EXPAND** the shape is right and the content is thin · **REDESIGN** the shape is wrong · **MERGE**/**SPLIT**/**REMOVE** structural.

Seven route files that no domain audited — sign-in, the signed-out stack, `more.tsx`, `projects.tsx`, `open.tsx`, `+not-found.tsx` and the root provider stack — are listed at the end of §2 with verdicts assigned by hand rather than by an auditor, and flagged as such.

## 4. Screens to create

The twelve domains between them named **148** screens the product should have and does not. Grouped by the domain that asked for them; duplicates across domains (a role home, a location browser and a real daily report were each asked for by four or more domains independently) are a signal of priority, not noise.


**Offline and sync UX**

- 'Available offline' (More > Available offline): everything this device holds — kept drawing sheets with revision and superseded flags, cached registers with their age and completeness, queued photos with total MB, and the free space left — with release and refresh actions and a running total. Today no screen in the app can answer 'how much of my phone is OctaPM using'.
- 'Get ready for site' / pre-download: pick a project, a location or a discipline and a date range, and pull down the drawings, the open punch list, the day's lookahead, the ITP templates and the team list in one tap with a progress bar. Right now preparing for a basement means browsing screens and hoping the cache kept them.
- A sync history / activity log: what was sent, when, from where, and what it became on the web ('16:20 · Punch · Chipped tile → PUNCH-114 · visible to the office'), with failures and their request ids. Sent items currently survive as at most 20 anonymous receipt rows and then vanish.
- A queued-item detail page: open one outbox row and see the entire captured payload — photos, location, activity, due date, assignee — edit it, fix what the server refused, re-queue it, or remove one bad photo without destroying the whole capture.
- An offline capability sheet ('What works offline'): a plain list of what can and cannot be done without a signal, reachable from the connectivity bar, so a site engineer learns the boundary before losing a half-hour of typing rather than after.
- A 'Kept offline' view of the drawings register (a filter chip at minimum): the sheets on this device, superseded ones first.
- A conflict screen: the outbox models CONFLICT (ATTENTION set in outbox.ts:64) and the UI shows only the server's sentence. When two engineers close the same punch item there is no side-by-side 'yours / theirs / keep which'.
- A sign-in screen statement of held work: 'You have 12 records captured on this device. Sign in as Ahmed Hassan to send them.' The queue survives a lost session and the person is never told.

**Procurement: orders, packages, long-lead**

- Deliveries due — a booking-in board of what is expected today and this week across all orders, with gate, time slot, and a tap straight into receiving. Today the only way to find an inbound delivery is to guess the PO.
- Suppliers list and supplier detail — contact card with tap-to-call and tap-to-email, lead-time reliability, trade categories, open orders, open alerts and delivery history. All of this data already arrives on every hub load and is discarded.
