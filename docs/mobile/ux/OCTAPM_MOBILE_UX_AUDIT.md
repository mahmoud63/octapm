# OctaPM Mobile — UX Audit

**Audience:** the product owner deciding the shape of Mobile UX V2, and the engineers who will build it.
**Date:** 2026-09-19. **Branch:** `feat/p0-foundations-sprint1`. **Method:** twelve read-only auditors over `mobile/`, each judging its domain as a construction professional would rather than as a developer; every gap each one found was then put to a second agent whose only job was to establish, from the backend source, whether the platform can already serve it. Two critics then looked for what the twelve had missed. Thirty-two agents, no failures.

**Everything below was re-verified by hand before being written down.** Where an agent was wrong, that is recorded in §6 rather than quietly dropped — one of its headline claims was wrong, and the correction matters more than the claim did.

## 2. The finding that reframes the phase

**The app is not missing a platform. It is not showing one.**

The backend serves, today, with permission guards and real data, a long list of things that have **no mobile screen and no mobile API call at all**:

- **The entire cost spine** — `/cost`, `/budget`, `/forecast`, `/cash-flow`, `/direct-costs`, `/budget-revisions`, `/accounting`. Two of the nine roles (`cost_controller`, `finance`) have essentially nothing to do in the app without it.
- **IPC certificates, sub-valuations, the prime contract** — the QS's core monthly deliverable.
- **Risks** — a named Phase 1a MVP module. Site staff are the people who first see a risk materialise and have nowhere to record it.
- **Submittals and specifications** — a field engineer cannot check whether the material on the truck matches an approved submittal.
- **ITP hold points and the defects register** — the actual construction quality gate. Inspections exist only as generic form instances.
- **BOQ and activity–BOQ allocations** — so quantity-based progress claiming, the normal way site progress becomes money, is impossible on a phone.
- **Schedule baselines, revisions and resources** — the Planning Engineer gets read-only activity rows.
- **Persisted drawing markups and pins** — `AnnotateSheet` burns strokes into a flattened JPEG and never POSTs to `markups.js`, so a markup cannot be edited, attributed or seen on the web.
- **Meetings, correspondence, transmittals, project email, equipment register, EOT and weather delays.**
- **Portfolio, reports, report builder, weekly reports and export** — a PM cannot send anything out of the app.
- **Six AI assistant routers and the conversation store.** The platform's stated differentiator is entirely absent from mobile.

And within the endpoints the app *does* call, it discards what it already receives: `PunchList.summary.byCompany` and `byTrade`, `summary.passRate` on material tests, `receipts[].lines` on a purchase order (typed `unknown[]` and rendered nowhere), every one of `submittedAt/approvedAt/acknowledgedAt/cancelledAt/closedAt` on a procurement order, and `item.resultId` — the id the server assigned to a queued record, which no screen ever shows, so the field can never prove the office has it.

`useDailyLogDays()` exists in `src/api/site-hooks.ts` **with no caller.**

## 3. The ten problems that decide the redesign

Ranked by what they cost a person on site. Each was verified by hand.

1. **One navigation for nine roles.** Five fixed tabs, sixteen module stacks hidden behind `href: null`, and a Home that is a wall of **six quick-action pills plus thirteen module buttons** with duplicate destinations (`/site` appears as both a module button and a quick action; `/photos` twice). Only six files in the app import an icon. This is the website's left-hand nav, flattened onto a phone.
2. **Progress updates and RFIs cannot be captured offline.** `OutboxKind` is exactly `punch.create | form.create | daily-log.entry | timesheet.create | attachment.upload | goods-receipt`. `activities/[id].tsx:95` calls `submit.mutateAsync` directly. The two most frequent field writes in the product fail without a signal — and that single fact is what sends an engineer back to WhatsApp.
3. **Progress approval is gated on screen width.** `activities/[id].tsx:80`: `const canReview = isExpanded && permissions.allowsTransition('progress', 'submission.approve')` — approval requires ≥900 dp. Needs You sends a PM to a submitted update on a phone and the record shows nothing to do and never says why. The app sends her there and then refuses her.
4. **Ninety of a hundred and seven screens cannot capture evidence**, against a brief that wants it on twenty-three record types — and against a backend that already accepts attachments on **fifty-two**.
5. **A photo never knows what it is of.** `photos/index.tsx:90` attaches every photo to `recordType: 'daily_log'`, and offline it fabricates a note entry reading "Site photos" to give them a parent. Nothing at capture asks what the photo shows.
6. **The drawing — the object a site engineer opens most — leaves the app.** `PdfPage.tsx`'s own header concedes Android cannot render a PDF and the sheet opens in the system viewer through the share sheet. No markup, no measure, no pin-drop. "Raise RFI from here" passes only `locationId`, so the RFI does not carry the sheet number, the revision, or where on the sheet.
7. **There is no day in the app.** Home's card labelled "Today" is an assignment counter — Overdue / Due today / Ball in court. The actual day's work sits three taps away behind a chip on the activities register, as a flat list with no crew, no location, no quantity target and no yesterday's figure.
8. **Queued work is invisible outside `/outbox`.** Not one screen imports `useOutbox` except the outbox itself. A punch item raised offline is absent from the punch register; a queued photo is absent from the gallery. The record the person just created disappears from where it belongs.
9. **The loop never closes back to the office.** `item.resultId` holds the id the server assigned and no screen uses it. Nowhere does a person read "this is now PUNCH-114 on the web register" — so the app never demonstrates the one thing the brief is about.
10. **Detail pages are thin and disconnected.** An activity that says "Inspection required" does not link to the inspection. Ninety-seven hand-rolled `Fact` renders and an eleven-fact 450 px "Details" card stand in for a record header. Related records — the thing that makes OctaPM a connected platform rather than twelve registers — are absent almost everywhere.

## 4. What is genuinely good and must survive

The audit was asked to find this too, and it did.

- **The drawings register** is the best screen in the app: search, discipline chips, favourites, recents, offline-kept state, and obsolete revisions marked *in the list* before you open them.
- **The inspection checklist** is a real one — answers held on device, saved in one request, a failed line raising a deficiency, a signature pad.
- **`ProgressSheet`** is well made: method-aware, quick 25/50/75/100 chips, percentage derived from quantity.
- **The approvals inbox** (`/workflow`) is the strongest PM surface: every step assigned to you, across projects, with due and overdue state, one tap to decide.
- **"Copy yesterday's crews and plant"** on the daily log is exactly the right idea.
- **Master–detail already works** through `SplitGroup` in eleven module layouts, and the width classes are driven by measured width rather than device names.
- **The offline queue, the permission model and the project switch are correct** and certified. None of this redesign may regress them.

## 5. Uncovered by the audit

The completeness critic found seven route files that no domain owned, and they are not minor: **the entire sign-in and credential experience**, the signed-out stack including the biometric `locked` state, `more.tsx` (the de-facto settings and profile screen), `projects.tsx` (the project picker and the multi-project mental model), `open.tsx` (the deep-link resolver, which silently switches the active project), `+not-found.tsx` (where every stale push notification lands), and the root provider stack.

They are added to the [page inventory](OCTAPM_MOBILE_PAGE_INVENTORY.md) with verdicts, and sign-in has already earned one finding by experiment: driving it in a browser, a mis-aimed tap on the password field hit **"Sign in with company SSO"** directly beneath it and navigated away from the app. Two adjacent 44 px targets on a 375 px screen with the destructive one below.

## 6. Where the audit was wrong, and the correction

Two of its claims did not survive verification. Recording them matters more than the claims did, because both would have sent the implementation somewhere expensive.

**"Offline is a promise the app does not keep" — wrong.** The product critic ranked this problem #1, arguing that `src/api/query.ts` builds a QueryClient with `gcTime: 24h` and **no persister**, so everything dies with the process while the connectivity bar claims otherwise. It read `query.ts` alone. `src/offline/persist.ts` is a bespoke persister — `restoreQueryCache()` and `startQueryPersistence()` — and `offlineSessionStarted()` calls both. `session.ts` invokes it on the password path (line 67) *and* on resume, biometric and restored alike (line 170). That is exactly the CERT-02 fix from the certification phase, and it works. **The read cache does persist across a restart.** Had this gone into the plan unchecked, UX2 would have opened by rebuilding a cache that already exists and by contradicting a certified fix.

What *is* true in that area, and stays: the 4 MB budget evicts the largest answers first — precisely the registers a field person needs; nothing can be pre-downloaded before losing signal; and queued work is invisible outside the outbox.

**"Constraints has no home" — my own error, not the audit's.** The first draft of the [site reporting design](OCTAPM_MOBILE_SITE_REPORTING.md) concluded that §14's constraints section could not be served and should be hidden. The audit found `GET /projects/:id/lookaheads/constraints` returning `{items, summary}` behind `lookahead.read`. It is corrected there: **seven of the eleven daily-report sections are native, four are derived, and none is missing.**

One smaller thing the app says about itself that is no longer true: `needs-you.tsx:80` tells every user "RFIs and tasks open in the app; other records open on the web until their screens arrive" — while `IN_APP_ROUTES` routes **twenty-two** record types in-app. The app is describing a version of itself that no longer exists.

## 7. How to read the per-screen audit

Each domain below lists its screens with the persona who uses it, what a construction professional cannot see, whether evidence can be captured, what happens on a tablet, and a verdict:

**KEEP** — leave alone · **EXPAND** — the shape is right, the content is thin · **REDESIGN** — the shape is wrong · **MERGE** / **SPLIT** / **REMOVE** — structural.

Then, per domain: the problems that span it, and the screens it should have and does not. The 212 needs the backend cannot serve are in the [page inventory](OCTAPM_MOBILE_PAGE_INVENTORY.md) with the honest option for each — derive, hide, or extend an existing model. **No section of this redesign invents data.**

**Across the domain**

- The app tells the person their offline work is safe when for most actions it is not. errors.ts:70 returns 'You are offline. What you save will be kept and sent when the connection returns.' for every OFFLINE failure, but only six OutboxKinds exist (punch.create, form.create, daily-log.entry, timesheet.create, attachment.upload, goods-receipt) against 60+ mutations in src/api. Progress submission, RFI responses, workflow approvals, NCR and safety closeout, punch transitions, form responses and signatures, comments, 
- Queued work is invisible outside /outbox. Not one screen in the app imports useOutbox except the outbox itself. A punch item raised offline is absent from the punch register, a daily-log entry absent from the day's log, queued photos absent from the gallery. The record the person just created disappears from where it belongs, which is exactly the behaviour that makes field crews stop trusting an app and go back to WhatsApp.
- There are three unconnected offline mechanisms with three vocabularies and no common home: the outbox ('waiting to send'), the read cache behind Freshness ('showing data from 2 hours ago') and kept drawings ('Kept offline at Rev C'). There is no single screen that answers 'what does this phone hold, what has not reached the office, and how much space is it using'.
- The loop never closes back to the web dashboard. item.resultId holds the id the server assigned and no screen uses it. Sending invalidates keys.project and keys.needsYou in senders.ts, so the office really does see the record, and the app never says so. Nowhere does the person read 'this is now PUNCH-114 on the web register'.
- Nothing can be prepared before going offline. The read cache in persist.ts is purely opportunistic — it keeps whatever queries happen to have settled — and its 4 MB budget silently evicts the LARGEST answers first, which are precisely the registers a field person needs (activities, the punch list). There is no 'download today's work', no per-location keep, and no warning that a list was dropped.
- The offline signal is rendered in the least legible way the design system permits: 13px white caption text on theme warning (#FDC047 dark, ~1.6:1 contrast; #B77E00 light, ~4.0:1) with no icon, no live region, and a layout that double-counts the top safe-area inset so every screen shifts down when it appears.
- Counts disagree. ConnectivityBar calls useOutboxStatus() unscoped while Home calls useOutboxStatus(projectId); the bar and the Home card can show different numbers for the same queue on the same screen, and neither states which projects it counted.
- Destructive recovery with no safety net and no repair path: Discard is a single unconfirmed tap that cascades to dependent photos, and a refused item cannot be edited — the only way to fix 'A title is required' is to throw the capture away and re-type it in the field.
- Files leak permanently. keepFilesForQueue copies every queued photo into documentDirectory/outbox/ and nothing deletes them — not discard, not the SENT_KEPT prune, not sign-out. Kept drawing PDFs in offline-sheets/ are orphaned by sign-out the same way. No screen shows this, no screen can reclaim it.
- A lost session is the scariest state and the least supported: endLocally() locks the outbox, wipes the read cache, and drops the person on the sign-in screen, which says nothing about the twelve records still held on the device. The outbox's own 'Sign in again to send what is waiting' subtitle has no button, and the layout redirects away before you can read it.
- No tablet thinking anywhere in the domain. Not one file in it calls useLayout; the outbox at 1024pt is a centred phone column.
- No photo capture anywhere in the domain, and the queued photos that exist cannot even be viewed.

**Screens this domain should have and does not**

- 'Available offline' (More > Available offline): everything this device holds — kept drawing sheets with revision and superseded flags, cached registers with their age and completeness, queued photos with total MB, and the free space left — with release and refresh actions and a running total. Today no screen in the app can answer 'how much of my phone is OctaPM using'.
- 'Get ready for site' / pre-download: pick a project, a location or a discipline and a date range, and pull down the drawings, the open punch list, the day's lookahead, the ITP templates and the team list in one tap with a progress bar. Right now preparing for a basement means browsing screens and hoping the cache kept them.
- A sync history / activity log: what was sent, when, from where, and what it became on the web ('16:20 · Punch · Chipped tile → PUNCH-114 · visible to the office'), with failures and their request ids. Sent items currently survive as at most 20 anonymous receipt rows and then vanish.
- A queued-item detail page: open one outbox row and see the entire captured payload — photos, location, activity, due date, assignee — edit it, fix what the server refused, re-queue it, or remove one bad photo without destroying the whole capture.
- An offline capability sheet ('What works offline'): a plain list of what can and cannot be done without a signal, reachable from the connectivity bar, so a site engineer learns the boundary before losing a half-hour of typing rather than after.
- A 'Kept offline' view of the drawings register (a filter chip at minimum): the sheets on this device, superseded ones first.
- A conflict screen: the outbox models CONFLICT (ATTENTION set in outbox.ts:64) and the UI shows only the server's sentence. When two engineers close the same punch item there is no side-by-side 'yours / theirs / keep which'.
- A sign-in screen statement of held work: 'You have 12 records captured on this device. Sign in as Ahmed Hassan to send them.' The queue survives a lost session and the person is never told.
